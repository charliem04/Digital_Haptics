If you run a trades business, you've probably gotten the calls and emails: someone promising to put you at the top of Google for a low monthly fee. Some are legitimate. Plenty aren't, and good businesses get burned every year. Here's how to tell the difference.

## Ask to see real work

Any web designer should be able to show you live sites they built. Open them on your phone. Do they load fast? Can you call in one tap? Would you trust that business? Bonus points if they've worked with trades and understand how your customers hire. You can see [ours here](/work/).

## Questions to ask before you pay

- **Who owns the domain?** It should be registered in your name.
- **Who owns the website and content?** Know what you keep if you leave.
- **What exactly does the monthly fee cover?** Get it in plain words.
- **Who writes the content and supplies photos?** This often catches people by surprise.
- **What happens if something breaks?** Know who to call and how fast they respond.
- **How will I know it's working?** Ask what you'll see each month.
- **Can I talk to a past client?** A confident designer won't mind.

## Red flags

Slow down if you hear or see any of these:

- A guarantee of the number one spot on Google
- Pressure to decide today
- A cold message claiming your site has "critical errors"
- Full payment up front, with nothing in writing
- No real examples you can visit
- Your domain or Google profile set up under their account
- Vague answers when you ask what you're paying for

## Get it in writing

It doesn't need to be complicated. A short agreement should cover what's being built, the price, the payment schedule, the timeline, what's included each month and who owns what when the work ends.

## Keep your own keys

Whoever you hire, keep access to the things that belong to your business:

- Your domain registrar login
- Your Google Business Profile, with you as the owner
- Your social media accounts
- Your hosting, or a clear way to move it

That way, if a relationship ends badly or someone disappears, your business doesn't lose its address on the internet.

## Cheap can get expensive

A low price isn't automatically bad, and a high one isn't automatically good. Compare what's included. A site you have to replace in a year, or one you lose access to, can cost far more than doing it right once.

If your website was built years ago, you've probably wondered whether it's time to replace it. Sometimes it is. Sometimes a few fixes will do. Here's how to tell before you spend anything.

## Start with five quick tests

Pull up your site on your phone and check:

1. **Can you call in one tap?** Your number should be obvious and clickable.
2. **Does it load quickly on cellular data?** Turn off Wi-Fi and try it.
3. **Is everything still true?** Check services, phone, hours, towns and photos.
4. **Does your form work?** Send yourself a test and make sure it arrives.
5. **Would you hire this company?** Look at it the way a stranger would.

## When fixes are enough

If the site is fast, works on a phone and looks reasonably current, you probably don't need a new one. Common fixes include:

- Updating services, photos and contact details
- Adding a clear call button and a working form
- Improving page titles so it can be found in search
- Adding pages for services or towns you're missing

## When a redesign makes sense

A redesign keeps the useful parts, like your domain and good content, and replaces how the site looks and works. It's usually the right call when:

- The site works, but looks dated next to your competitors
- It's hard to use on a phone
- It doesn't reflect the quality of your work anymore
- Your business has grown past what it shows

## When to start over

A full rebuild is worth it when the foundation is the problem:

- It's very slow and no fix seems to help
- It runs on an abandoned platform or nobody can update it
- You can't get the logins, or a past developer controls it
- It doesn't work properly on phones at all

## Protect what you've already earned

If your site has been online a while, it may have search history worth keeping. Replacing it carelessly can cost you. Before any rebuild:

- **Keep your domain name.** It's yours, and it carries your history.
- **Redirect old pages** to their new versions so links and rankings aren't lost.
- **Keep content that's working** rather than throwing everything out.
- **Make sure you have access** to your domain, hosting and Google profile.

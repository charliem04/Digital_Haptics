When someone is choosing between two roofers they've never heard of, reviews often settle it. The good news is that most happy customers are willing to leave one. They just need to be asked, at the right time, with as little effort as possible.

## Ask right after the job

The best moment is when the customer is looking at finished work and feeling relieved. A week later, they've moved on. Make it part of how you close out a job, the same as cleaning up and collecting payment.

## Make it one tap

Nobody is going to search for your business and hunt for the review button. Google Business Profile gives you a direct review link. Save it on your phone so you can text it in seconds.

## What to say

Keep it short and personal. Something like:

> Thanks again for having us out, Ms. Landry. If you were happy with the work, a quick Google review would really help our small business: [your link]

Using their name and sending it yourself works better than an automated message from nowhere.

## Make it a habit for the whole crew

If you have a crew, let them know reviews matter. A customer who liked the person on their roof is often glad to say so. Asking them to mention what was done and which town they're in also makes the review more useful to the next person reading it.

## How to answer reviews

Reply to all of them when you can. For good reviews, a quick, specific thank-you is plenty.

For bad reviews, take a breath first. The person you're really writing for is the next customer reading it:

- Stay calm, and don't argue the details in public
- Acknowledge the problem and say you want to fix it
- Offer a way to talk directly
- Keep any private job details out of your reply

A professional answer to a bad review can build more trust than a page of perfect ones.

## What never to do

These can get reviews removed or your profile penalized:

- Buying reviews or posting fake ones
- Offering discounts or gifts in exchange for a review
- Reviewing your own business, or having employees do it
- Only asking customers you think will be positive

If a review breaks Google's policies, such as spam or a person who was never a customer, you can flag it for removal. Google makes the final decision, and a review isn't removed just because it's negative.

## Keep them coming

A steady trickle of recent reviews looks better than a burst from three years ago. Ask every time, and it adds up.

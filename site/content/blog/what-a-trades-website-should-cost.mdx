Ask ten web people what a website costs and you'll get ten answers. That's frustrating when you're just trying to run a business, and it makes it easy for the wrong person to take advantage of you. Here's how to think about it.

## What actually changes the price

A site's price mostly comes down to how much work it takes:

- **Number of pages.** A one-page site is a very different job from a 20-page site with a page for every service and town.
- **Features.** Booking, financing tools, galleries you can edit and hiring forms all add build time.
- **Writing and photos.** If someone has to write your content or organize your photos, that's real work.
- **Locations.** Serving more towns usually means more pages, done properly.

## One-time cost and monthly cost

These are two separate things, and you should know exactly what each covers.

The **build** is the work of making the site. The **monthly** cost usually covers some mix of hosting, maintenance, updates and local SEO. Before you agree, ask: what do I get each month, and how will I know it's being done?

## What you should own

This is the most important part of this article. No matter who builds your site:

- **Your domain name should be registered in your name**, with a login you control.
- **Your Google Business Profile should belong to you**, even if someone else manages it.
- **You should be able to leave** and take your domain and content with you.

If someone else holds these and disappears, you can lose your web address and start over. It happens to good businesses more often than it should.

## Warning signs

Be careful with anyone who:

- Guarantees you the number one spot on Google. Nobody can promise that.
- Wants the full payment up front and won't put the scope in writing.
- Registers your domain in their own name.
- Can't show you real sites they've built that you can visit.
- Charges a monthly fee but can't tell you what it pays for.
- Reaches out of nowhere saying your site has "critical errors" and pressures you to act today.

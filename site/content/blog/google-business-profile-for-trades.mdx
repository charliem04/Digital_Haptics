When someone searches for a plumber or roofer nearby, the map and business listings are often the first thing they see. Those listings come from Google Business Profile. It's free, and for a local trade it may be the most useful thing you can set up in an afternoon.

## Check whether you already have one

Search your business name on Google. If a listing appears, it may have been created automatically or by someone who used to work with you. Look for the option to claim or manage it rather than making a second one, because duplicates cause problems.

## Create the profile

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and sign in with a Google account you'll keep long term. Use a business account if you have one, and make sure the owner always has access. Don't let the profile live only in a contractor's or web person's account.

- **Business name:** use your real name, exactly as it appears on your truck and paperwork. Adding keywords like "Best Roofer Lafayette" breaks Google's rules and can get a profile suspended.
- **Category:** choose the one that most closely describes your main work, like Roofing contractor or Plumber. You can add a few secondary categories if they're true.
- **Phone and website:** use the same number and business name everywhere you appear online.

## If you work out of a truck

Most trades are what Google calls a service-area business: you go to the customer. When asked whether customers can visit your location, answer no. Your home address stays hidden, and you list the towns or areas you serve instead. Keep that list to places you really work.

## Get verified

Your profile won't fully appear until Google verifies it. Google chooses the method, and it can vary by business. A short video showing your signage, truck, tools or proof of business is common, so have those handy. Follow the instructions closely, and make sure the name on your proof matches the profile.

## Fill out everything

An empty profile looks abandoned. After verification, add:

- Your hours, including how you handle emergencies, if you do
- Each service you offer
- A short, plain description of your business
- Real photos of your crew, equipment and finished jobs

## Ask for reviews

Reviews help customers choose you. The easiest time to ask is right after a job goes well. Google gives you a link you can text to the customer. Don't pay for reviews, offer discounts in exchange for them or post fake ones. That's against Google's policies and can cost you the listing.

Reply to your reviews too, including the bad ones. A calm, helpful answer tells the next customer a lot about how you run your business.

## Keep it current

Update your hours, add new photos every so often, and fix anything that changes. A profile works best alongside a website that says the same things.

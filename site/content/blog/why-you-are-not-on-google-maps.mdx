You search for your trade in your town and your competitors show up on the map, but you don't. It's frustrating, and it usually comes down to one of a handful of causes.

## How Google decides

Google says local results are based mainly on three things:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone searched
- **Distance:** how close you are to the person searching or the place they named
- **Prominence:** how well known your business is, including reviews and what's said about you online

You can't move your business closer to everyone, but you can improve the other two.

## First, check how you're searching

Results change with where you're standing. If you search from your driveway and then from across town, you'll get different answers. Being invisible in one spot doesn't mean you're invisible everywhere. Try searching from where your customers actually are.

## You don't have a profile, or it isn't verified

No profile means no map listing. And a profile that hasn't finished verification may not appear at all. Sign in at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and check its status. If you've never set one up, start with our [setup guide](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/).

## Your profile is suspended

Google can suspend listings that seem to break its guidelines, often for keywords in the business name or an address that doesn't qualify. If you see a suspension notice, fix the problem before requesting reinstatement.

## Your category is wrong

If your main category is a loose match, you may not show up for the searches that matter. A roofer should be listed as a roofing contractor rather than a general category. Look at what the top local competitors use.

## Your profile is thin

Missing services, no photos and no description give Google little to work with, and give customers little reason to call. Fill out everything that's true.

## You have few or old reviews

Reviews are a big part of prominence. If competitors have a steady stream and you have three from years ago, that's a clear place to catch up. Here's [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## Your information doesn't match

If your name, phone or website differ across the web, it weakens confidence in your listing. Clean up old directories and make everything say the same thing.

## You have a duplicate listing

Two profiles for one business can compete with each other or get flagged. Search for variations of your name and old phone numbers, then report or merge any duplicates.

## You don't have a website behind it

A profile linked to a clear website gives Google more to understand about what you do and where. If you're running on a Facebook page alone, read [whether that's enough](/blog/facebook-page-or-website/).

## Be patient, and skeptical

Changes can take time to show up. And anyone promising you the number one spot is promising something they don't control.

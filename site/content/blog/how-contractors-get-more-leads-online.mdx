Most contractors don't have a marketing problem. They have a few small gaps between a customer searching and the phone ringing. Close them in the right order and you get more from what you already have, before you spend a dollar on ads.

## 1. Make sure you can be found by name

Search your business name on your phone. If a referral looked you up right now, what would they see? You want your own website and a complete Google listing at the top. If you see an old address, a dead phone number or nothing at all, start here. Word of mouth only works if people can follow it to you.

## 2. Set up your Google Business Profile

For local trades, this is usually the fastest win. It's free, and it's what puts you on the map when someone nearby searches for your trade. Our guide on [setting up a Google profile](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/) walks through it.

## 3. Have a site that turns a visit into a call

A search only helps if the page it lands on does its job. Your number should be one tap away, your services should be obvious, and there should be a short form for people who can't call right now. Here's [what a trades website actually needs](/blog/what-a-trades-website-needs/).

## 4. Answer the phone, or answer fast

This one costs nothing and matters more than almost anything else on this list. Someone with a leak or a damaged roof will often call the next company if they get voicemail. If you can't pick up on a job, call back as soon as you're off the ladder. Check your form emails through the day, too.

## 5. Collect reviews as you go

Reviews help you show up and help people choose you. Make asking part of finishing a job. We cover how in [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## 6. Publish what customers search for

Once the basics are solid, add pages that answer real questions: what a job costs, how long it takes, what to do in an emergency. [Coastal Construction Services](/work/coastal-construction-services/) has guide pages and a flood zone lookup for land buyers, which gives people a reason to find the site before they're ready to hire.

## 7. Then think about ads

Ads can work, but they send paid clicks to whatever you've already built. If your site is slow or your listing is empty, you're paying to show people that. Fix the foundation first.

## What this can look like

[Darren Aucoin's Plumbing](/work/da-plumbing/) didn't need anything elaborate. A clear one-page site with a lead form has brought in more than 30 new leads since launching in May 2026.

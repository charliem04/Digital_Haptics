Plenty of good trades businesses run on a Facebook page and word of mouth. It's free, it's easy, and your customers are already there. So do you really need a website?

For most businesses that want new customers, yes. But you don't have to give up Facebook, and you don't need anything big.

## What Facebook does well

Facebook is good for staying in front of people who already know you. You can post finished jobs, share updates after a storm and let happy customers tag you. In a tight-knit area, that goes a long way.

## Where it falls short

The trouble starts when someone who has never heard of you needs a plumber right now.

- **People search Google for this.** A Facebook page isn't built to show up when someone searches for your trade in your town.
- **You don't own it.** Facebook sets the rules, can change how the page looks and can restrict your account.
- **It's hard to scan.** A customer in a hurry has to dig through old posts to learn what you do and where you work.
- **It goes stale.** You're busy. If your last post is from two years ago, a stranger may wonder whether you're still in business.
- **Some people don't use it.** Anyone without an account can run into login prompts just trying to look at your page.

## What a simple website adds

A website is a home base you control. It tells a stranger who you are, what you do and how to reach you, in about ten seconds, and it can show up when they search. It also doesn't go stale the way a feed does, because it isn't sorted by date.

It doesn't have to be a big project either.

## A real example

[Darren Aucoin's Plumbing](/work/da-plumbing/), based in Scott, had a Google Business Profile and a Facebook page that had gone quiet. We built one clear page with the company's services and a lead form that goes straight to email. Since launching in May 2026, it has brought in more than 30 new leads.

## Use them together

The best setup is usually all three:

1. A **website** as your home base
2. A **[Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/)** so you show up on the map
3. A **Facebook page** for staying in touch, with a link to your website

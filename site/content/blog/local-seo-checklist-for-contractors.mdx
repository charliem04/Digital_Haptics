Local SEO is the work that helps your business show up when someone nearby searches for your trade. It sounds technical, but most of it is getting the basics right and keeping them that way. Use this as a checklist.

## Your Google Business Profile

- The profile is claimed, verified and owned by you
- Your name matches your real business name, with no added keywords
- Your main category is the closest match to your core work
- Your services and service areas are filled in
- Your hours are correct, including how you handle emergencies
- You have real photos of jobs, equipment and your crew

## Your website

- Your business name, phone number and service area are easy to find
- Each main service has its own section or page
- Page titles say what you do and where, like "Roof repair in Lafayette, LA"
- The site loads quickly on a phone
- Call buttons and forms work, and you've tested them yourself
- The site is secure, with `https` in the address

## Your name, address and phone everywhere

Search engines compare what different sites say about you. If your phone number differs between Google, Facebook and an old directory, that creates doubt, and it can send customers to a dead line. Pick one exact version of your business name and number, then use it everywhere.

## Directories that matter

You don't need hundreds of listings. Start with the big ones customers actually use, plus any trade or local associations you belong to. Be wary of anyone selling bulk directory submissions.

## Reviews

- You ask every happy customer, soon after the job
- You have a direct link ready to text
- You reply to reviews, including the bad ones
- You never buy reviews or offer rewards for them

More on that in [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## Pages for the towns you serve

If you work across several towns, a page for each can help, but only if each one says something real about your work there. Copying one page and swapping the town name is a common shortcut that adds little value.

## Mistakes that hold contractors back

- Stuffing keywords into your business name
- Having two Google profiles for one business
- Listing towns you don't actually work in
- Letting a web person own your domain or profile
- Paying for a guaranteed first-place ranking, which nobody can promise
